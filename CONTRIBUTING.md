# How to Contribute

Thank you for being here. ODCF only exists if people other than its convener shape it.

**You do not need to know Git, code, or law to contribute.** Thoughtful comments are as valuable as finished text.

---

## Ways to contribute, from easiest to most involved

### 1. Comment on an open question
Go to the **Issues** tab, find a question that interests you (start with [OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)), and add your thoughts. Short comments are fine. Disagreement is welcome.

### 2. Raise something new
If something is missing, wrong, unclear or naive, open a new issue. Use one of the templates:
- **Comment on RFC 001**: feedback on a specific section
- **New open question**: something the framework needs to answer
- **Precedent or existing effort**: an institution, project or study we should learn from
- **Translation**: offer to translate or report a translation problem

### 3. Propose a change to the text
If you are comfortable with Git, open a **pull request** that edits the document directly. If you are not, paste your suggested wording into an issue and someone will help turn it into a change.

### 4. Write a new RFC
For a substantial new idea, such as a full governance model, an alternative strategy, a technical architecture or a funding model, write a new RFC:

1. Copy [`rfcs/0000-template.md`](rfcs/0000-template.md) to `rfcs/0000-your-short-title.md`.
2. Fill it in. Incomplete drafts are welcome. Mark them as such.
3. Open a pull request. It will be given a number when it is merged as a draft.

RFCs can **compete**. Two contradictory proposals on the same question are healthy; discussion decides between them.

### 5. Join a Working Group
Each Working Group has a page in [`working-groups/`](working-groups/). Introduce yourself in the WG's pinned issue and say what you would like to work on.

### 6. Translate
See [translations/README.md](translations/README.md).

### 7. Spread the word
Share RFC 001 with people who should read it: researchers, NGOs, journalists, public officials, open-source communities. Tell us (in an issue) who you shared it with and how they reacted.

## How decisions are made (for now)

During v0.x:

- Small fixes (typos, links, clarity) can be merged by any maintainer after a quick review.
- Substantive changes to RFC 001 stay open for **at least 14 days** for discussion before merging.
- Contested changes are not merged until disagreement is either resolved or clearly recorded in the text (for example, as two alternative options).
- Nobody, including the convener, merges their own substantive changes.

See [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) for how this process is meant to evolve.

---

## Writing guidelines

- **Write for an intelligent non-specialist.** Explain jargon or link to an explanation.
- **Be concrete.** "Assembly of 150 randomly selected members serving two-year terms" is more useful than "a democratic body".
- **Name your uncertainty.** "I think…", "One option is…", "This needs checking" are all welcome.
- **Cite sources** for factual claims, especially about laws, institutions and existing projects.
- **Argue with ideas, not people.** See the [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
