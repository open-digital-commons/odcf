# WG 3: Civic Deliberation, Safety and Moderation

**Status:** Looking for founding members and a coordinator

## Purpose
Design how ordinary citizens take part in governing the commons, and how people are kept safe, without recreating opaque, centralised control.

## Key questions
- How is a Citizen Assembly selected, how large is it, how long do members serve, and what binding powers does it hold? (Q14)
- How can moderation work at scale, across languages and cultures, with fair appeals? (Q15)
- How do we protect people from harassment, manipulation and illegal content without central surveillance? (Q16)
- What role could citizen juries play in difficult moderation or policy decisions?
- How do we include people who are less online, less technical, or outside Europe and North America? (Q17)

## First deliverables
1. A short review of **citizens' assemblies and juries** (for example Ireland's Citizens' Assembly and other deliberative processes): what worked, what didn't, what transfers to a digital commons.
2. A **layered moderation model**: what is decided by users, communities, regional bodies and the global institution.
3. A first draft of **Articles IV–VI** of the charter (Assembly, powers and disputes, moderation and appeals).
4. A list of safety risks specific to a public, government-funded platform (for example targeted political pressure, state-backed manipulation).

## Useful backgrounds
Deliberative democracy, political science, trust and safety, content moderation, human rights, online harassment research, community management, sociology.
