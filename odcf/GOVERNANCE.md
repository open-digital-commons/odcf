# Governance of This Project

ODCF proposes democratic, capture-resistant governance for public digital infrastructure. It should practise what it proposes, starting with how this repository is run.

This document describes how the **ODCF project itself** is governed. It is not the proposed governance of any future platform; that is discussed in [RFC 001](RFC-001.md).

---

## Principles

1. **No owner.** The ideas and texts belong to the commons (CC BY 4.0). No person or organisation owns ODCF.
2. **Openness.** Discussions and decisions happen in public, in this repository.
3. **Hand-over by design.** The founding arrangements are temporary and come with an exit plan.
4. **Record disagreement.** When consensus is not reached, the disagreement is written down rather than hidden.

---

## Phase 0: Convening (now)

**Convener:** Afif Say.

The convener's role is **procedural, not directive**:

- open the repository and the initial discussion;
- invite participants, especially experts and civil-society organisations;
- keep the process fair, welcoming and moving;
- appoint the first additional maintainers from among active contributors.

The convener **does not**:

- have a final say on content;
- merge their own substantive changes;
- hold the role permanently.

## Phase 1: Shared stewardship

**Trigger:** at least five regular contributors from at least three countries.

- A **Stewardship Group** of 5–9 people is formed from active contributors, with attention to diversity of country, discipline and background.
- Each Working Group selects one or two **coordinators**.
- The convener becomes an ordinary member of the Stewardship Group, or steps back entirely.
- Maintainer rights on the repository are shared among stewards and WG coordinators.

## Phase 2: Formal structure

**Trigger:** RFC 001 reaches v1.0, or institutions request a formal counterpart.

The Stewardship Group proposes, through a public RFC, whether ODCF should:

- remain an informal open project;
- join an existing non-profit as a fiscal host;
- or establish its own non-profit association.

That RFC follows the normal process, with an extended public comment period of at least 30 days.

## Decision-making

| Type of change | Process |
|---|---|
| Typos, links, formatting | Any maintainer, after quick review |
| Substantive change to a document | Open ≥14 days; merged by a maintainer who is not the author |
| New RFC accepted as a draft | Merged when complete enough to discuss; acceptance is not endorsement |
| Change to this governance document | Open ≥30 days; requires broad support with no sustained objection from active contributors |

**Lazy consensus** is the default: if a proposal has been visible for the stated period and no one has raised a substantive objection, it may proceed. Sustained objections are resolved through discussion; if that fails, both positions are recorded.

## Conflicts of interest

Anyone acting for a government, company, political party or funder must say so when contributing on matters related to that interest. Undisclosed conflicts may lead to removal of maintainer rights.
