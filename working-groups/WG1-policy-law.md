# WG 1: Policy, Law and International Governance

**Status:** Looking for founding members and a coordinator

## Purpose
Design the legal and institutional structure that lets democratic states fund and protect public digital infrastructure **without being able to control it**.

## Key questions
- Which legal form fits best: international non-profit association, foundation, federation of national bodies, or treaty organisation? (Q6)
- How can a charter make the infrastructure impossible to sell, buy or capture? (Q5, Q22)
- How should conflicts between different countries' speech and content laws be handled? (Q7)
- How do states, cities, universities and NGOs join, participate and leave? (Q9)
- Which institutions (European Commission, national parliaments, Council of Europe, others) are the right first audiences, and what would they need to see?

## First deliverables
1. A short comparison of 3–4 legal forms, with their strengths and weaknesses for this purpose.
2. A case-study note on how independent public bodies (public broadcasters, CERN, central banks, others) are protected, and where that protection has failed.
3. A first draft of Articles IX–XI of the charter (membership, amendment, dissolution).
4. A two-page policy brief summarising RFC 001 for public officials.

## Useful backgrounds
International law, EU law, non-profit law, constitutional law, public policy, diplomacy, digital rights advocacy.
